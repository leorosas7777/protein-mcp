24. `open_poses_in_pymol(uniprot_id, structure="", pocket="", compounds=None, start=True)` (Phase 3, step 10b, 2026-11-19): `files/viewer.py`. No network. One receptor and one object per docking run of the same pocket, **one colour per compound** (the colour is identity, never order), plus the pocket and the box. Every poses file is checked against its SHA-256 before being loaded; state 1 of each object is the best-scoring pose and the rest are in the same object.
25. `render_md_movie(uniprot_id, structure="", pocket="", stage="", every=0, width=960, height=720, fps=12, keep_frames=False)` (Phase 3, step 10c, 2026-11-19): new module `files/movie.py` (pure function + CLI `python -m protein_mcp.files.movie`). No network. The already recorded trajectory → an `.mp4` beside the PDFs: PyMOL in batch (`-cq`) ray-traces one PNG per state and the ffmpeg binary of `imageio-ffmpeg` encodes them. `every=0` picks the sampling so that the call returns soon, and declares it. The video carries **in its own metadata** the run, the sampling, the fact that the seconds are playback and not simulated time, and the equilibration control of that run.
19. `analyze_admet(uniprot_id, structure="", pocket="", conformer="", pose=1, compound="")` (Phase 3, step 7, 2026-11-11): new module `files/admet.py` (pure function + CLI `python -m protein_mcp.files.admet <ACC> --structure 3OG7 --pocket "032 C"`) and a new block at the end of `sources/chembl.py`. ## 6. Known limitations and open bugs
- **RCSB, a false negative on the server side:** RCSB's `full_text` search does not separate the gene from the variant, so entries with an empty mutation field and the variant only stuck in the title never reach the set (verified 2026-10-20: 9GLU "KRasG12D-GDP…" and 5UFQ do not appear when searching for "G12D"). Our confirmation step would recognise them. Possible fix: add a search on `pdbx_mutation`, or on the title with gene prefixes, to the OR; not validated.
- Size sent to the chat (real tests 2026-10-20/21): `get_ligands` KRAS G12C 37.1 → 15.4 KB, PCSK9 13.7 KB, EGFR 63 KB (see below); `search_literature` 10 articles 23.6 KB and 25 articles ~53 KB (deliberately not compacted: they are the abstracts); `get_structures` 5.8 KB; `get_protein_profile` 10.8 KB (dossier 318 KB).
- `get_protein_profile`: Open Targets' "cancer or benign tumor" area includes some syndromes (e.g. linear sebaceous naevus, ALPS4 in KRAS); only the first 500 associations are examined; with several Ensembl genes the first one that has a target in Open Targets is used and the rest are not queried (if two genes had a target, only one would be seen; not observed); OMIM answers 403 to automated clients (the link `omim.org/entry/{MIM}` works in a browser, not checked here).
- `get_ligands` with truncation (EGFR, 20,949 activities): only the 6,000 most potent are seen (pChEMBL ≥ 7.94) plus those of compounds with a mechanism; for the other compounds, `n_measurements`, the median and the worst value only count what is above the cut, and with a mutation filter the ones for that variant below the cut are lost (`assay_variant_mutation` does NOT accept a server-side filter: `icontains` errors out). EGFR takes 36–64 s depending on ChEMBL's load (2026-10-21; a risk of timeout in Claude Desktop, not checked) and the compact view weighs 65 KB (51 ligands: 15 + 36 with a mechanism). Inclisiran (siRNA) does not appear for PCSK9: its mechanism is not on target CHEMBL2929.
- `get_ligands`: ranking by best pChEMBL favours single measurements; `max_phase` is only checked on the compounds returned and on those with a recorded mechanism, so other clinical compounds with no mechanism may not appear (the `max_phase__gte` filter on molecule is NOT validated). The PDB ligands are not cross-referenced with ChEMBL. A ChEMBL assay can be mislabelled in the opposite direction (text with no mutation named and the wrong label): that is not detected.
- **`assay_class` depends on ChEMBL filling in `assay_cell_type` (verified against the real API on 2026-11-17).** `_assay_class` (`chembl.py:271`) marks "cellular" only if the assay record carries `assay_cell_type`. The classifier does the right thing —the opposite hypothesis was tested and was false— but **the field comes back empty for assays whose description does name the cell line**, and then a cellular assay lands in the biochemical group. Three real cases inside the V600E / IC50 / binding group of vemurafenib (CHEMBL1229517 against CHEMBL5145): `CHEMBL4194742` (32 nM), `CHEMBL4367952` (80 nM) and `CHEMBL4230004` (127 nM) say "in human A375 cells" and carry an empty `assay_cell_type`; `CHEMBL3371773` (33.1 nM) and `CHEMBL3578338` (150 nM), of the same kind, do carry it (`A-375`) and are classified as cellular. Since cellular values are systematically worse (permeability, ~1 mM intracellular ATP, binding to proteins in the medium), the misclassified ones **widen the bad end of the biochemical group** and the range the researcher reads is not homogeneous. Possible **unvalidated** fix: check the assay description against the field, as is already done with the mutation (`mutation_label_conflicts_description`, 21 rows excluded in this same query).
- **The `best/median/worst` ranges are NOT comparable between compounds when the download was truncated (verified against the real API on 2026-11-17).** With truncation, the compounds **with a recorded mechanism** additionally receive the **complete** download of their activities (`activities.mechanism_compounds_download: "complete"`), and the rest keep only what is above the cut. In P15056/V600E the cut was pChEMBL 7.23 (≈ 59 nM): dabrafenib, sorafenib, vemurafenib, RAF-265 and LY-3009120 (with a mechanism) show their real range —factors of 4 to 22,000— while CHEMBL525191 and SB590885 (with no mechanism) **cannot show any measurement worse than 59 nM**, so their narrow range is not reproducibility but the cut (the giveaway: SB590885's cellular group is 28/58/58 nM, right against the cut). Comparing ranges is only legitimate within the same completeness group. The two fields needed are already in the JSON (`activities.mechanism_compounds_download` and `ligands[].mechanism`), but neither the report nor the notes warn about it.
- RCSB mutation filter: only the best 100 by resolution of those RCSB finds by text are examined; the confirmation step may discard entries that name the variant only in other fields (they are listed in `discarded_not_confirmed`).
- With a mutation filter, several structures come back with no small-molecule ligand; the nucleotide analogues (GDP/GCP/GNP) take up slots (pending: group them as GDP vs GTP analogue).
- 7ROV and 6WGN are bound to peptides (they appear in `partner_chains`, `ligands: []`); counting polymeric binders as ligands is pending.
- The DOI of 7T47 is a preprint (Research Square).
- AlphaFold: the `link` is built with the accession that was queried, not with the `entryId` that was chosen; if an isoform is chosen, the link opens the canonical model while the figures belong to the isoform (found by the reviewer 2026-10-21, not corrected).
- "Not found" paths: resolved on 2026-11-04 (task 9, section 4, tools 3 and 4). What remains: the check costs one extra request to UniProt per call; if UniProt does not answer we carry on with an `accession_warning` (an empty result then proves nothing); and in a DEMERGED entry the error copies the whole `mergeDemergeTo` list, where one of them may itself be inactive (Q96D10 → P01116 and P01118, and P01118 is merged; found by the reviewer, not corrected: the datum comes from the source and the next attempt gives a clear error). Isoforms (`P01116-2`) are refused earlier by `parse_accession`. P99999 is NOT usable as a non-existent accession: it is human cytochrome c.

It includes neither abstracts nor the interpretation. It depends on Arial (Windows); on another OS another TTF with Unicode has to be registered. An old dossier from another version of the code might lack a field ("no record" is shown).

The measured block is almost always empty and that is the source, not a defect: vemurafenib, an approved drug, has 4 rows in this project's allowlist (ChEMBL has more ADME rows that the list does not admit: see below) and TXV none. Only assay types A and P are queried, so an ADME measurement filed under B, F, T or U does not appear (what was not queried is named, but it is not searched). **hERG and the rest of off-target safety are not there**: vemurafenib has `AC50 > 30000 nM` against CHEMBL240 with `assay_type=F`, and it was decided to leave that for 7.1. The `ADME_TYPES` allowlist is a choice of our own (32 entries after the review) over a ChEMBL vocabulary that is not controlled (`Papp` and `permeability` coexist; in the sample of 3,000 rows of `assay_type=A`, `Saluretic potency` and `Hammett constant` appear): an ADME type that is not on the list is discarded, counted in `rows_excluded_by_allowlist_by_standard_type`. **The list is narrower than ADME and the output says so**, never "it is not ADME": the two `Ratio` rows it discards for vemurafenib are efflux ratios of **P-gp** (21.7) and **BCRP** (15.2), and one of its `Activity` rows is **CYP450 induction** (a reduction in the AUC of midazolam), which are textbook ADME. Measured as well over 5,000 rows of `assay_type=A` (143 distinct `standard_type` values): the list lets 40.5 % of the rows through. `ADME_PAGE = 20` is set because `limit=1000` and `limit=200` return HTTP 500 **three times out of three** against the real API; the 500s with small pages are intermittent and are absorbed by the retry in `_get`, but a `ReadTimeout` with ChEMBL loaded leaves the block UNKNOWN (this happened during development). Now it only says that ChEMBL returned `-1` and that it is not interpreted. The other half of the same asymmetry is covered too: the flags that come back `False` in an unannotated entry (TXV: 6 of them, along with 7 fields at `-1`, `max_phase` None and no ATC) are listed in `boolean_fields_false`, and the report warns that a `False` there is **not** proof that the compound is not oral or was not withdrawn: the API does not distinguish "annotated as no" from "not annotated". The two calculation engines (RDKit and ChEMBL) can disagree and **there is no way to say which one is right**; on the two real ligands they agree to the hundredth, which proves nothing. The rules in 7.3 are conventions with no retrieved source. ### 7.2 Pre-registration of the MD interpretation thresholds (written 2026-11-11, BEFORE any production trajectory existed)
> Translated from the Spanish original written on 2026-11-11. The original wording is preserved unchanged in git history (commit `c1d0191` and earlier). Translation made on 2026-11-23, when the repository moved to English; no figure, parameter or criterion was altered.

It is written in advance for the same reason as 7.1, and the occasion is an honest one: job 20070766 is running and **not one** of the 10 chunks has finished. The only thing measured as this is written is the **250 ps of equilibration** of 3OG7/vemurafenib. **Do not modify this subsection**: if a threshold changes it is recorded in section 8 with the reason and the date.
- **What they are:** the four thresholds against which `reading()` (tool 18, `analyze_md`) compares what was measured. They are **conventions in common use, not measurements of this system and not values derived from any source that was retrieved**. No literature is cited because none was consulted: attributing them to "the literature" with no source was an unsupported claim and it has been removed from the code.
- **The thresholds, with their reason:**
  - **Plateau of the protein backbone: drift ≤ 0.5 Å.** It is the equilibration control, and below that figure the drift is of the order of the thermal noise of a backbone at 303 K itself. Measured as the **mean of the last quarter minus the mean of the FIRST quarter**, not against the overall mean: for a straight ramp of height h that second form only reaches 0.375 h, so a protein rising 1.2 Å without flattening would have passed a 0.5 Å test (a defect found by the reviewer on 2026-11-11 and corrected).
  - **Ligand RMSD: ≤ 2.0 Å** over the last quarter. It coincides numerically with the docking-validation threshold of 7.1 **by convention, not by derivation: it measures something else** (there, a calculated pose against a crystallographic pose; here, the persistence of one pose over time). The same number is kept deliberately so as not to multiply criteria, and it is stated that the coincidence is not an argument.
  - **Range of the centre-of-mass distance: ≤ 2.0 Å.** It separates reorientation inside the site from dissociation. The order-of-magnitude reference is the user's thesis (neuropeptide-GPCR, 10 ns), where the stable systems oscillated in ranges of ~2 Å and the least stable one in ~3 Å. It is a scale reference from a DIFFERENT system, not a transferable value.
  - **Persistent hydrogen-bond occupancy: ≥ 0.5** of the frames analysed. Half the sampling is the conventional cut between a contact that holds the binding and an occasional one. **It is not a fraction of time**: hydrogen bonds break and re-form within picoseconds and the DCD stores one frame every tens of picoseconds, so it is a sampling of the occupancy and the output says so.
- **What is expected and what would be a finding:** with a pose that reproduces the crystal to 0.54 Å, the expectation is that all four are met. **If they are NOT met, the finding is about the MD setup, not about the pose**, and that is exactly what this positive control is for. As this is written the equilibration values pass all four with a wide margin (drift 0.066 Å; max. RMSD 0.53 Å; COM range 0.26 Å; occupancies 92/72/44/36/16 %), and **that validates nothing**: it is 250 ps with restraints, not 10 ns of production.
- **How they are presented:** never as a measurement. `reading` goes in a separate block with `status: "READING OFFERED, NOT MEASURED"`, lists `thresholds_used`, and its `disclaimer` says that they are conventions and that the researcher's judgement decides. No metric carries a verdict of its own (there is a test that demands this).
- **Scope decisions taken on 2026-11-11, with the user:** **no apo control** in this phase (it is reconsidered when new ligands are tested on the same receptor, where the question becomes whether the ligand changes the protein); **no bridging waters** in this first version; the analysis is run **locally**, not on the cluster; and the file transfer stays **manual** (WinSCP or `rsync`/`scp`), with no client connected to the project, for consistency with `build_md_job`, which deliberately connects to no cluster, and so as not to put credentials into the server.

### 7.3 Pre-registration of the ADMET rules (written 2026-11-11, BEFORE writing any code for step 7)
> Translated from the Spanish original written on 2026-11-11. The original wording is preserved unchanged in git history (commit `c1d0191` and earlier). Translation made on 2026-11-23, when the repository moved to English; no figure, parameter or criterion was altered.

It is written in advance for the same reason as 7.1 and 7.2. **Do not modify this subsection**: if a rule changes it is recorded in section 8 with the reason and the date.
- **Honest scope of the date** (a lesson from the second correction to 7.2, which called a docstring "too strong"): this does **not** rule out that the rules were adjusted to a result already seen for the project's two ligands. As this is written I have already read from the real API `alogp 5.54` and `num_ro5_violations 1` for 032/vemurafenib and `alogp 5.78`, `num_ro5_violations 1` for TXV, so **I know in advance that both break Lipinski on the logP**. What the date does rule out is adjusting them against a **future** compound, which is the tool's real use.
- **The three rules, with their cut-offs exactly as implemented:**
  - **Lipinski:** MW ≤ 500, cLogP ≤ 5, HBD ≤ 5, HBA ≤ 10. The **number of violations** is reported, not "pass/fail": the original rule already allows one.
  - **Veber:** rotatable bonds ≤ 10 and TPSA ≤ 140 Å².
  - **Egan:** TPSA ≤ 131.6 Å² and cLogP ≤ 5.88.
- **No literature is cited, for the same reason as in 7.2:** in this project **no publication has been retrieved** for these rules. The names "Lipinski", "Veber" and "Egan" go in as **labels in common use, not as citations**, and the cut-offs are the ones written above, not "the ones in the literature". If one day they are to be cited, the source is retrieved with `search_literature` and recorded here.
- **What the rules are NOT:** they are not a prediction of bioavailability, of absorption or of toxicity. They are property filters on a molecule, and an approved drug can break them (vemurafenib breaks them and was approved in 2011, a fact that is in the tool's own RECORD block). The output has to be able to say both things at once without their reading as a contradiction.
- **Two different calculations of the same quantity, and they are not crossed.** The descriptors are calculated with **RDKit on the exact SDF that was docked and simulated**; ChEMBL additionally returns its own (`molecule_properties`: `alogp`, `psa`, `qed_weighted`…), calculated with **another engine**. They go in separate rows, each labelled with its engine, and they are **never averaged or presented as mutual confirmation**: two calculations that agree are not a measurement, and if they disagree there is no way to say which one is right. That ChEMBL's `heavy_atoms: 33` matches the 33 heavy atoms of the SDF is a check of **identity**, not of properties, and that is the only thing it is used for.
- **How they are presented:** the three rules go in a separate block with `status: "READING OFFERED, NOT MEASURED"`, their `rules_used` list with the literal cut-offs, and a `disclaimer` saying that they are conventions and that the judgement is the researcher's. The same pattern as `reading()` in `analyze_md`, and no property carries a verdict of its own.
- **The three provenances do not share a block** (the rule from 7.1): `RECORD` (facts from the ChEMBL record: phase, approval, withdrawal, routes, ATC), `MEASURED` (retrieved ADME activities, each with its literal unit and its assay description) and `CALCULATED` (RDKit and, separately, ChEMBL). "Predicted" is **left unused in step 7**: no number in this version comes from a trained model. The word is reserved for step 7.1 and the text in `experiment_report.py` that already announces it has to be corrected.

## 10. Working protocol
- One task per session (in Claude Code) or one chat per step (on claude.ai); the role is stated at the start.
- Always test against the real APIs; the simulated tests must reflect real defects.
- Keep replies short; show changes as diffs except in large refactors; do not paste the whole of `server.py` unless it is needed.
- One commit per step, made by the user (or by Claude Code if explicitly asked); do not version backup files (`server_old.py`), `dossiers/` or test output (`probe.txt`).
- Tests: the `tests/` folder, run with `uv run python tests/<file>.py`. Real tests: `tests/smoke_live.py`.
- After changing a tool: the offline tests + the matching `smoke_live.py` group + the `revisor` agent before the commit.
